# Form, wordmarks and lockups

## Choosing the form
The categories — wordmark, letterform, emblem, pictorial, abstract — blur, and none is right by rule (Wheeler pp. 48–49). The branch is the name and where the mark lives:

- **A short, distinctive name → a wordmark** alone is often enough (Identify, Introduction; Dime).
- **A long or unwieldy name, or parts to unify → a symbol** that stands in for it; a long wordmark would have to shrink into small spaces (Identify, HarperCollins; Merck; Chase Manhattan).
- **A generic name → treat one part of it visually** to make it distinctive (Identify, Showtime; Centro).
- **Initials → only when people already use them**, and never one letter pulled out of an acronym people say whole (Identify, EPA; NBC).
- **A letterform mark** is a mnemonic and sits in an app tile easily (Wheeler p. 54).
- **An abstract mark** buys ambiguity for a broad or technical offer and is the hardest to do well; it gains meaning through exposure (Wheeler p. 58; Identify, Chase Manhattan).
- **A pictorial mark** when it must be read fast in a cluttered setting (Identify, Old Chatham).
- **An emblem** (name locked inside a shape) is the hardest to read small — a poor fit where the favicon is the main use (Wheeler p. 60). A containing disc is different: it shields the mark from busy backgrounds (Identify, PBS; WTA).
- **Beside other marks it would fight** — sub-brands, partner logos — drop the symbol (Identify, Hearst).

## Wordmarks
- **Simple lettering plus one memorable device** — the red O, the dot, the check (Identify, Mobil; Dime). A legible word with distinctive letter traits may absorb a pictorial or abstract element (Wheeler p. 52).
- **Hand-tune it.** Spacing, stem weight and stroke width are where a set word becomes a mark; the tweaks go unnoticed and make the effect (Identify, Barneys; Showtime).
- **Echo the symbol's geometry in the letters** (Identify, NBC; Rockefeller Center). A face with a meaningful origin beats a neutral one when one exists (Identify, Library of Congress; PBS).
- **Stacking or a divider forces the right reading** of a name that misreads run together (Identify, MIAD; SEW Infrastructure).

## Lockups
- **The signature is a fixed relationship** of mark, name and any tagline, in horizontal and vertical versions (Wheeler p. 48). Fix the size ratio between symbol and name once and derive every lockup from it (Identify, National Geographic).
- **Secondary names sit smaller and lighter** beneath or beside the primary, aligned to the mark (Identify, Merck; Smithsonian; Hearst).
- **The app icon** takes the abstract mark, the letterform, the shorthand, or a wordmark only if it stays legible at tile size (Wheeler p. 75; Identify, El Mall).
